### 2a. The Petersson identity that is actually PRESENT (not for Ξ)
In BF’s notation, k is half-integral with k≠1, κ=2−k, f∈H^+_(k,L), and g∈M_(κ,L^−), with the stated Fourier expansions and convergence/cusp hypotheses. Their Prop.3.5 states

    {g,f} = (g, ξ_k(f))_(κ,L^−)
          = Σ_(h∈L#/L) Σ_(n≤0) a^+(h,n) b(h,−n).

For g cuspidal this is the ordinary convergent Petersson pairing with the cusp shadow ξ_k(f); BF’s quotient pairing is nondegenerate. This is a quotation of a general source formula, **not** an identity for Ξ. In particular no f, g, coefficients a^+, b, or lattice representation is assigned to Ξ here. The conventional ξ_k(f)=2iy^k overline(∂f/∂barτ) is antilinear and changes the representation to its dual. Signs, conjugations and normalizations must be retained from the relevant source.

The weight pair (1/2,3/2) occurs in BF and explicitly in Kane–Man. That establishes a relevant literature host, not that project Ξ is a cusp shadow or even a holomorphic modular form. Cone shadows of weight-one theta completions can instead be real-analytic products; Funke–Kudla explicitly treats this broader phenomenon. BOR’s integer-weight D^(k−1) theorem must not be transplanted to k=3/2.

### 2b. Three different uses of “Petersson”
1. BF: pairing of a modular g with the ξ-image of a harmonic Maass preimage; finite principal-part formula.
2. BOR/Borcherds: specified regularized integrals where ordinary convergence fails. BOR §4 expressly warns that regularized products need not exist for two arbitrary weakly holomorphic forms with genuine cusp poles.
3. Knightly–Li: a spectral **trace formula** over a Hilbert eigenbasis. It does not construct a project shadow, its preimage, or a boundary vector for C.
These are not interchangeable identities.
