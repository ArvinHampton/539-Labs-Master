## Fix 5. SU(2)_R: the false finite-subgroup clause. AGREE: RETRACTED and replaced (log [F5])

- **Correct argument, PROVED.** For the k = 3 representation, x, y and z = (xy)⁻¹ have orders 2, 3 and 8.
  - Finite subgroups of SO(3) with an element of order 8 are only cyclic or dihedral; T, O and I have none.
  - In a cyclic or dihedral image, the elements of orders 3 and 8 lie in the rotation part about one axis. Then x = (yz)⁻¹ lies there too, so all three are coaxial.
  - For coaxial rotations, xyz = 1 forces the rotation angle of z to be −(π ± 2π/3), so z has **order 6, not 8**. Contradiction.
  - So the image is infinite. The same coaxial argument excludes SO(2) and O(2) as its closure, so the closure is SO(3).
  - Independently, the y-axis tilt of 1.113098 rad is neither 0 nor π/2.
- **Cyclic and dihedral images in general** (PP's request) reduce to cases already handled:
  - A cyclic image is abelian: ac257bf7 §2, only the trivial connection is invariant.
  - A dihedral image with a unique axis: b0c6e848 §2, REFUTED by H¹(Σ;ℤ₂)^{Aut⁺} = 0.
  - A V₄ image: b0c6e848 §3.
