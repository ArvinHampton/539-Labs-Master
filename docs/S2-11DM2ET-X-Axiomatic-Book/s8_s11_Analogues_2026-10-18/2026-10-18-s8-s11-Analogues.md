## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

- **s = 8 behaves like s = 7.** The whole S/T family is Eisenstein, so ψ = ψ_Eis. Ψ equals the genus-character Herglotz closed form, checked to about 3e-13 at 5 complex points and 8 rationals.
- **s = 11 breaks part of the mechanism.** Everything up to the Eisenstein Herglotz piece carries over. But u_L (the principal row) has a genuine **dihedral cusp component**: 6 Maass cusp forms with λ = 1/4, coming from 12 non-genus ray-class characters of conductor (3) and (9). So ψ = ψ_Eis + ψ_cusp with ψ_cusp ≠ 0, and the s = 7 statement "ψ is purely Eisenstein / Herglotz" is **not family-wide**.
- **Structural reason (Cat A for the counts, interpretive as an explanation).** The relevant narrow ring class group equals its genus quotient for s = 7 and s = 8, but not for s = 11:

| s | discriminant | h⁺ | genera |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 140 | 4 | 4 |
| 8 | 48 | 2 | 2 |
| 11 | 396 | 8 | 2 |

## 1. Fundamental-domain identity (Cat A)

The identity 2P_s = E_s + 1 + θ₃ − Θ_hol,s holds exactly as a q-series to **q^3000** for s = 7, 8 and 11 (`series_s.py`, output `series_s.txt`, all PASS).

## 2. Field, unit, γ, ray-class structure

Notation: Q_s(n,k) = Q₂(ν) + c₀, with ν = (n,k) + a. Here Q₂ = x² + sxy + (s/2)y², a = (s/(2(s−2)), −1/(s−2)), c₀ = s/(4(s−2)), γ = [[−1,−s],[2,2s−1]], and β_X = (2X₁+sX₂) + cX₂√D₀, with N(β_X) = 4D²Q₂.

| | s=7 (reference) | s=8 | s=11 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field | Q(√35) | Q(√3) | Q(√11) |
| Order of the form | maximal, h = 2 | Z[2√3] (conductor 2), h = 1 | Z[3√11] (conductor 3), h = 1 (Minkowski bound 3.3; 2 ramified with 𝔭₂ = (3+√11); 3 inert) |
| Unit ε | 6+√35 | 2+√3 | 10+3√11 |
| γ eigenvalue | ε | **ε²** | ε |
| Multiplicity of E as a sum over ideals | 1 | **1 (no double counting)** | 1 |
| Minimal modulus of E | 𝔭₂𝔭₅∞∞, one class of 16 | (2√3)∞∞ = 𝔭₂²𝔭₃, group of order 4; E-set = C₀ ∪ 𝔭₂·H | 𝔭₂·(9)∞∞, one class (22,0) in Z/24×Z/2 (order 48) |
| Norm map | N𝔞 = 20Q₂ | N𝔞 = 3Q₂ | N𝔞 = 36Q₂ (N𝔞 ≡ 25 mod 36) |

Notes on the table (Cat A unless marked):
- **s = 8, γ = ε².** Even so, the cone [e₁, γe₁) is a fundamental domain for ⟨ε²⟩ = U_{𝔪,1}, the totally positive units ≡ 1 mod 𝔪. So each ideal is counted exactly once; this is what the multiplicity-1 row records. The s = 8 E-set also contains 𝔭₂-multiples, which is new: they come from the non-maximal order.
- **Multiplicity and counts** come from `rayclass_s.py` (outputs `rayclass_s_7_8_11.txt` and `.json`). The PARI ideal enumeration matches the counts to norm 3000 with maximum difference 0. Minimal moduli come from `minmod_s.py` (outputs `minmod_*.txt`; for s = 11 the check runs to N𝔞 ≤ 15000).
- **Hand checks** of the ray class number h·φ(𝔪)·4/[U:U_{𝔪,1}]:
  - s = 8: 1·4·4/4 = 4. Here ε ≡ −1 mod √3 and ε² ≡ 1 mod 𝔪.
  - s = 11: 1·72·4/6 = 48. Here ε ≡ 1 mod 3, ε³ ≡ 1 mod 9, but ε ≢ 1 mod 9.
  - s = 7: 2·4·4/2 = 16.
  - All three agree with PARI bnrinit.
- **Genus and periodicity test** (`genus_test.py`, `prime_periodicity.py`; the latter uses no PARI). I compared E_s counts at primes p < 60000 by residue class:
  - s = 7 (mod 840) and s = 8 (mod 240): **0 residues vary**, so the counts are genus-periodic.
  - s = 11 (mod 1980): **20 residues vary**, for example p ≡ 97, 313, 421, 709. So s = 11 is not genus-periodic and the class set is not a union of H = (1−σ)Cl⁺_𝔪 cosets: σC = C + d with d = (12,0).

## 4. Theta lift, S/T family, Eisenstein/cusp split

**Representation data (Cat A; `family_s.py`, outputs `family_s_7_8_11.txt` and `S_s/T_s/P_s.npy`).** For all three s: S is unitary, S² = P and (ST)³ = I. So the P-invariant part is a representation of PSL₂(Z).

**Eisenstein/cusp split** (Cat A; `content_s.py`, `analyze_content.py`, `raw_rank.py`, `cusp_chars_11.py`, `cusp_dim.py`):
- **s = 7 (validation).** u_L is pure Eisenstein. The label space splits as 60 Eisenstein-or-zero + 12 cusp, matching the earlier report. The function space splits as 36 Eisenstein + 6 cusp.
- **s = 8.** All 48 components are pure Eisenstein, checked at moduli 2⁷3² and 2⁹3³ (192 classes). **The cusp part is 0.** The 18-dimensional image is entirely Eisenstein.
- **s = 11.**
  - **u_L has |Eis| = |cusp| = 0.204** in content norm. Mixed-type content is ≤ 4e-15 at the original run and 7e-17 after σ-symmetrisation.
  - The cusp characters of u_L are 12 non-σ-invariant pure-type characters at 𝔭₂(9). They give **6 dihedral Maass forms (λ = 1/4)**: 3 of type (0,0) and 3 of type (1,1), each with content |F̂| = 1/12.
    - Conductor (3), norm 9: characters of order 4, giving level 44·9 = 396.
    - Conductor (9), norm 81: characters of order 12, giving level 44·81 = 3564.
  - The whole family has cusp function-space rank **≥ 73** inside the 110-dimensional image, so the Eisenstein part has dimension ≤ 37.
  - By conductor norm, the family contains ≥ 90 dihedral newforms (45 per type): 9: 1+1; 81: 2+2; 99: 4+4; 396: 10+10; 891: 8+8; 3564: 20+20.
  - These are **lower bounds**: 84 of 396 components were never covered by the enumeration (deep cosets).

## 5. ψ cocycle

Convention (as in the s = 7 `complex_check.py`): Ψ(z) = E_L(−1/z) − (z/√|det|)·E_R(z), with cone sums over [e₁, γe₁).

**Herglotz template (Cat A numerically; Cat B as a proof for s = 8 and 11).** The Eisenstein part has the same closed form for every s:

  Ψ_Eis(z) = Σ 2F̂·R(z),  R(z) = Σ_m a(m) Σ_s ρ_s·ψ_Γ(s/N_b + λm/z).

Here the genus characters are χ = ψ∘N with a = ψ and b = ψχ_D, and η_aη_b = χ_D(−1) = 1. The script `herg_s.py` identifies the genus ψ automatically.

| s | check | result |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Ψ vs Herglotz | 2e-13 (8 characters) |
| 8 | Ψ vs Herglotz, 5 complex points (`herg_8.txt`) | **3e-13** |
| 8 | exact rational ψ(x) vs Herglotz, x ∈ {1, 2, 1/2, 1/3, 3/2, 2/3, 3/4, 5/3} (`rational_check_8.txt`) | **≤ 3.9e-13** at all 8 |
| 11 | Ψ − Ψ_nonEis vs Herglotz_Eis, 4 complex points (`ecusp_11.txt/.log`) | **2.5e-13** at all 4 |
| 11 | Eisenstein-only Herglotz vs full Ψ | misses by ~1e-2 |

Details:
- **s = 8 Herglotz terms:** λ = 1/3 (F̂ = ±1/4, characters (0,0) and (1,1)) and a **dilated λ = 2/3 piece** (F̂ = 1/2, 1/2), with ψ-type characters mod 12. The dilated piece is new: it comes from the 𝔭₂-multiples in the E-set.
- **s = 8 rational values:** ψ(x) is computed exactly (Euler–Maclaurin/Bernoulli κ, `kappa_s.py`). The same code reproduces the s = 7 value ψ(1) = 0.0068839937604 − 0.0382597122170i.
- **s = 11 split:** the σ-symmetrised non-Eisenstein content of both E_L and E_R is purely pure-type (mixed ≤ 4e-16), on exactly the same 12 characters (2,6,10,14,18,22; ·). Ψ_nonEis(i) = 0.0031849 − 0.0035444i, which is nonzero.
- **Proof template.** The s = 7 lemmas and the Liouville step are generic, given three inputs:
  - (I1) the genus Lambert decomposition;
  - (I2) the S-law of the family;
  - (I3) the continuation.
  - For s = 8 all three hold numerically, so a proof would go through the same way (Cat B, not re-audited).
  - For s = 11 the template proves only the Eisenstein projection; the cusp projection needs a separate argument.

**Not done: the L(0)-value identities for the s = 11 cusp rows.** These would express ψ_cusp(h/k) through additive-twist L(0,χ) values. The obstacle is the bad-prime C-part tower: at the constant term every C-part contributes with no decay, so the identity needs a closed form for the high-valuation class functions G_Cp at 2, 3 and 11. I did not attempt this within the budget.

One standard fact limits which characters can contribute: nonprincipal totally even (0,0) characters have L(0,χ) = 0 (a double trivial zero), so only the 3 forms of type (1,1) can contribute L(0) terms at x = 0-type points. I am stating this, not checking it here.

## 6. What carries over, what differs, what is new

**Carries over exactly (Cat A), for all s:**
- the fundamental-domain identity;
- E as a multiplicity-1 single-class ray-class generating function;
- rational ζ_E(0) and ζ_E(−1), with t^{±1/2} terms vanishing;
- the log-unit residue and the Baker obstruction;
- the S/T family relations, giving a PSL₂(Z) representation;
- the Herglotz closed form for the Eisenstein part.

**Differs:**
- **s = 8:**
  - γ ↔ ε², but with no double counting;
  - non-maximal order of conductor 2, so 𝔭₂-multiples enter the E-set and a dilated Herglotz term appears;
  - **even** lattice, so the family is the standard Weil representation.
- **s = 11:**
  - order of conductor 3, so h⁺ = 8 > 2 genera;
  - not genus-periodic;
  - **u_L and ψ have dihedral cusp parts.**

**New phenomena:**
- The cusp/Eisenstein dichotomy is governed by whether the narrow ring class group equals its genus quotient. This holds for s = 7 and 8 and fails for s = 11. Cat A for the counts; the explanation is interpretive.
- s = 8 has a √π-rational t^{−1/2} term.
- s = 11 has a large cuspidal image: ≥ 73 dimensions and ≥ 90 dihedral newforms.

## 7. Caveats

- Several results are finite-range checks: q^3000, primes below 60000, the stated norm bounds, and about 1e-13 numerics.
- s = 11 cusp dimensions and newform counts are lower bounds, because some deep cosets are uncovered.
- The ray-class, L-function and character computations depend on PARI. Known PARI quirks were worked around by hand: bnrmap returns primitive characters; lfun uses primitive Euler factors.
- The diagnostic `ecusp_8` run (modulus 2⁷3²) is **inconclusive**, not a failure. The E_R phase is not a class function at that modulus (6692 inconsistencies). The s = 8 result rests on `herg_s` and `rational_check` instead.
