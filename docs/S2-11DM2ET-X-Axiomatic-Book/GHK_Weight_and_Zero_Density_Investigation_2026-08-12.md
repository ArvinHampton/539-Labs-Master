## 1. Zero-density status at moderate sigma

The programme requires, for O-M1.2 and for the density input to the majorant Phi of Direction 3, a classical bound on N of beta-star comma T that is at most a polylogarithm of T at some fixed moderate abscissa beta-star lying between 0.55 and 0.80.

Literature check (August 2026):

- Guth–Maynard (announced 2024, published 2026) improve the uniform zero-density exponent from Huxley’s classical 12/5 = 2.4 to 30/13 approximately 2.3077. This is the first substantial improvement in the key range in more than eighty years and yields short-interval primes down to length x to the power 17/30 plus epsilon. The resulting zero-density exponent A of sigma remains of the form constant times (1 minus sigma) and therefore still produces a positive power of T at every fixed moderate abscissa.

- The Analytic Number Theory Exponent Database compilation (arXiv 2501.16779) records the current best A of sigma piecewise:

  - for sigma less than or equal to 0.7 the Ingham bound 3 over (2 minus sigma) governs;
  - for 0.7 less than or equal to sigma less than 0.76 the Guth–Maynard bound 15 over (3 plus 5 sigma) governs;
  - higher ranges carry various classical or optimised bounds, none of which reduce the power of T to a polylogarithm at fixed moderate sigma.

- Explicit versions (Chourasiya–Simonič 2025, Bellotti 2024–2025) improve logarithmic powers and absolute constants, and give strong control very near sigma equals 1 (including an absolute-constant bound when sigma is sufficiently close to the Korobov–Vinogradov edge). These improvements do not convert the positive T-power into a polylogarithm at moderate abscissae.

Conclusion for the programme: the classical density-gap table at moderate sigma remains frozen. No post-2025 result supplies N of beta-star comma T less than or equal to a polylogarithm of T at any fixed moderate abscissa between 0.55 and 0.80 under technology admissible under the zero-density language architecture. Direction 1 and the density input to O-M1.2 therefore stay blocked by the same positive-power obstruction.

## 2. GHK hybrid weight and the joint-window obstruction

In the programme the GHK hybrid remainder appears in two places:

- in the far-sum control that must be balanced against the GHK contribution inside the numerical majorants for O-M1.2;
- as the hybrid term inside the template-level majorant Phi of Direction 3 and as the offline error that Direction 4 requires in a thin vertical strip.

Under the programme constant c1 equal to 291 and at the reference height H_RH equal to 3 times 10 to the 12, the joint window between far-sum control and GHK control is empty. That is, the classical density exponents currently available do not leave room for both error terms to be made simultaneously smaller than the target remainder.

A successful refinement of the GHK weight would have to achieve one of the following concrete numerical effects while remaining inside classical technology:

1. Reduce the effective constant or the support width of the hybrid remainder so that, under the same classical density exponents, a non-empty interval of heights appears in which both the far-sum error and the GHK error fall below the programme target.

2. Supply a usable bound on the GHK error in a thin strip of the form sigma belonging to the closed interval from one-half to one-half plus delta-zero, of size much less than the square root of X over log X (or any explicit multiple that the offline conversion can absorb).

Neither effect is presently recorded as a classical theorem usable as programme input. Smoothing the cutoff, changing the weight function, or restricting the effective strip are legitimate avenues for further classical work, but they have not yet produced a bound that opens the joint window or closes the offline obstacle.
