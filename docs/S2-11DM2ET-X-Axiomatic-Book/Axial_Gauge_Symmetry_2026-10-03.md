# Axial gauge symmetry

## Two meanings

An axial gauge condition is a choice of representative, n · A = 0, used to fix a redundant description. An axial gauge symmetry is an invariance, A_mu to A_mu + partial_mu lambda, under which the action does not change. The names share a word. They are not one object.

## The term already written

The field strength F_mu nu is invariant under A_mu to A_mu + partial_mu lambda. The mass term (1/2) mu^2 A_mu A^mu is not. A nonzero mu breaks the gauge symmetry explicitly. The Proca field then has three degrees of freedom, not two.

Setting mu = 0 restores the symmetry and is a second stipulation. The massless theory needs a gauge condition, and the axial gauge n · A = 0 is one such condition. Restoring the symmetry does not derive mu. It deletes the mass.

## Coupling to spin

A gauge-invariant coupling of A_mu to an axial fermion current is j5^mu A_mu. That current is not conserved in a quantized theory with vector couplings. The Adler-Bell-Jackiw anomaly is a failure of partial_mu j5^mu = 0, not a torsion. It does not source T^lambda_mu nu on the lock, and it does not select the spin census.

On the lock the five-piece stress has no spin density, so the current in the field equation is zero whether or not the gauge symmetry is present. A = 0 remains a solution. Gauge symmetry does not raise the near-field scale 2.26 times 10^{-39} per metre.

## Split

Gauge condition, gauge symmetry, and the axial anomaly stay three. The mass term breaks the symmetry. The lock does not use the symmetry. L1 through L7 are unchanged.
