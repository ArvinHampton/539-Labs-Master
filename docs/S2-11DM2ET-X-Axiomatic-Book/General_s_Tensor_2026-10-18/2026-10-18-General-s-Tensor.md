## Statement
Setup: s ≥ 5 odd, A = [[2,s],[s,s]], n = s(s−2) (odd, gcd(s, s−2) = 1), a = (s/(2(s−2)), −1/(s−2)), w_c = A⁻¹(0,½), γ = [[−1,−s],[2,2s−1]].

**(1) Basis.**
- L′/Z² ≅ Z/n is cyclic, generated by g = A⁻¹e₂, because gcd of the entries of A is 1.
- a = a₂ + a_odd with a₂ = (½,0) and a_odd = (1,−1)/(s−2) ∈ L′.
- In the basis u′_{x,ε,δ} := u_{xg+εa₂, −δa₂} (x ∈ Z/n, ε,δ ∈ {0,1}), with N = 4n labels:
  - T = diag_x e(−x²/n) ⊗ T₂;
  - S = [e(−2xy/n)/√n]_{x,y} ⊗ S₂;
  - S₂: (ε,δ) ↦ (δ,ε) with phase (−1)^{εδ};
  - T₂: 00 ↔ 01, 10 ↦ i·10, 11 ↦ i·11.
- S₂ and T₂ do not depend on s: they are the 2-adic theta-characteristic factor.

**(2) Local factors.**
- By CRT, Z/n = ⊕_{p^e ‖ n} Z/p^e, and −x²/n = Σ_p q_p(x_p) with q_p(x) = −v_p x²/p^e, v_p = (n/p^e)⁻¹ mod p^e.
  - For p | s: v_p⁻¹ ≡ −2·(s/p^e).
  - For p | s−2: v_p⁻¹ ≡ 2·((s−2)/p^e).
- With ρ(S) := S⁻¹ (the label convention, as at s = 7) and ρ(T) := T, ρ = ⊗_p ρ_{q_p} ⊗ ρ₂.
  - ρ_{q_p} is the Weil representation of (Z/p^e, q_p).
  - ρ₂ = (e(3/4)·S₂⁻¹, T₂), which is the s = 7 factor, now for every odd s.

**(3) Negation and γ.** Both act exactly as monomial maps on u′:
- negation: x ↦ −x, phase 1, i.e. ⊗_p Z_p ⊗ I₄;
- γ: x ↦ (s−1)x with phase (−1)^{εδ}, i.e. ⊗_{p|s} Z_p ⊗ ⊗_{p|s−2} I ⊗ diag(1,1,1,−1).

**(4) Dimensions.**
- P = S² = Z_n ⊗ I₄ exactly, so dim W = dim Fix(P) = 4·(n+1)/2 = 2(s−1)².
- Fix(N) ∩ Fix(γ) = {Z_(s)Z_(s−2) = +1, Z_(s)·Z₂ = +1}, where Z₂ = diag(1,1,1,−1). Its dimension is 3·E(s)E(s−2) + O(s)O(s−2) = s(s−1), with E(m) = (m+1)/2 and O(m) = (m−1)/2.
- The number of forced-zero labels is (s−2) + s − 1 = 2s − 3.
- dim V ≤ s(s−1), with equality wherever the rank is certified.

## Proofs
**Step 1: factorisation. Cat A (symbolic, all odd s).** Facts used:
- B(g,g) = (A⁻¹)₂₂ = −2/n, so Q(g) = −1/n.
- B(g,a₂) = 0; Q(a₂) = ¼; B(a₂,(m₁,m₂)) = m₁ + s·m₂/2.
- w_c = g/2.
- n·g = (s,−2), so (n/2)g = a₂ + c with c = ((s−1)/2, −1) ∈ Z².

*T.* α + β + w_c = (x+h)g + (ε−δ−1)a₂ − c, with h = (n+1)/2.
- The new characteristic is δ′ = δ+1−ε mod 2, and the remainder μ = (x+h)g + (ε−δ−1+δ′)a₂ − c lies in L′.
- Phase e(B(α,μ)) = e(−2x(x+h)/n + ε/2), using B(xg,c) = −x ∈ Z and B(a₂,c) = −½.
- Prefactor: −Q(α) − B(α,w_c) = x²/n − ε/4 + x/n.
- Sum: −x²/n + x(1−2h)/n + ε/4 = −x²/n + ε/4 (mod 1), since 1−2h = −n.

*S.* With p = yg: p − β = yg + δa₂, so ε′ = δ. Then β′ = α = −εa₂ + (xg + 2εa₂), so δ′ = ε.
- Phase e(B(yg+δa₂, xg+2εa₂)) = e(−2xy/n) (the εδ term is an integer).
- Prefactor e(B(α,β)) = e(−εδ/2).

*CRT split.* 1/n = Σ_p v_p/p^e, and −x²/n ≡ Σ −v_p x_p²/p^e.
- Each (Z/p^e, q_p) is nondegenerate: v_p is a unit and p is odd.
- The Fourier kernel factors as e(−2xy/n) = ∏ e(−2v_p x_p y_p/p^e), the bilinear form of q_p.
- So S⁻¹ ∝ e(−b) and T = e(q): the standard Weil formulas.

*Exact verification.* The Fractions computation, entry by entry on all 4n·n entries of S and all of T, together with the negation and γ maps, agrees with the symbolic formulas at s = 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 21, 25 (8/8, `matches_symbolic`), including the prime powers 9 = 3² and 25 = 5².

**Step 1b: congruence. Cat A, by citation, with the hypotheses checked.**
- *Weil scalar.* The scalar is γ_n = e(1/4) for all odd s. This holds exactly: n = s(s−2) ≡ 3 mod 4 always, and Gauss's evaluation Σ_{x mod n} e(x²/n) = ε_n√n with ε_n = i gives Σ e(−x²/n) = −i√n. Numerically γ_n = e(2/8) at all 12 tested s.
- *2-adic factor.* So ρ₂(S) = e(3/4)·S₂⁻¹ for every odd s. It satisfies S⁴ = I and (ST)³ = S² = −I exactly (Fractions, all 12 s), and factors faithfully through SL₂(Z/4) (exact enumeration, 48 elements, Tensor-Pattern-Closure; S₂ and T₂ do not depend on s).
- *Odd factors.* Theorem (Nobs–Wolfart 1976 for p-groups; Skoruppa; Strömberg, "Weil representations associated with finite quadratic modules", Math. Z. 275 (2013)): for a nondegenerate finite quadratic module of odd order and level N, the Weil representation is a genuine representation of SL₂(Z) that factors through SL₂(Z/N). For orthogonal sums it is the tensor product of the local ones.
  - Hypotheses checked: each (Z/p^e, q_p) is nondegenerate, of odd order, of level p^e; the formulas match exactly.
- *Conclusion.* ρ factors through SL₂(Z/4) × ∏SL₂(Z/p^e) = SL₂(Z/4n), so the label count and the order of T are N_s = 4s(s−2), confirming N11.
  - Caveat: I checked the citation's convention against the exact s = 7 relations, but not against the published text line by line.

**Step 2: negation and γ. Cat A (symbolic).**
- *Negation.* −(xg+εa₂) ≡ −xg + εa₂ mod Z². The β shift μ = δ(1,0) gives phase e(B(−xg+εa₂, δ(1,0))) = e(εδ) = 1.
- *γ.* γ ∈ SO(A) gives γg = A⁻¹γ^{−T}e₂ = A⁻¹(−2,−1) = (s−1)g − A⁻¹(2,s) = (s−1)g − e₁, and γa₂ = a₂ + (−1,1).
  - The β shift μ = δ(1,−1) gives phase e(δ(−(s−1)x + ε(1−s/2))) = e(εδ/2), i.e. (−1)^{εδ}.
- *Local action.* Locally, s−1 ≡ −1 mod p^e for p | s and ≡ +1 for p | s−2. This is ε_s = (s−1)+√(s(s−2)) reduced at the ramified primes. Verified exactly at all 8 s.
- The function identities used are u_{−α,−β} = u_{α,β} and u_{γα,γβ} = u_{α,β}; the latter holds by the γ-orbit construction of the components.

**Step 3: dimensions. Cat A for the upper bounds and W.**
- *P.* P = S² = Z_n ⊗ I₄ exactly: (F/√n)² = Z_n because 2 is invertible mod n, and S₂² = I. So dim W = 2(s−1)².
  - Correction: the guess "dim W = 3∏(p^e+1)/2" in the task is the Eisenstein dimension, not W.
- *Upper bound.* dim V ≤ s(s−1) from the sector formula. The orbit-cocycle count on the explicit maps (weil_sectors) gives exactly s(s−1) at 12 odd s, and the number of zero labels is 2s−3.
- *Lower bound.* Cusp-Onset-Closure's mod-ℓ ranks (vrank_*.json, `certified_V` = True) equal s(s−1) for every odd s from 5 to 49 and at s = 51 (Q45). So **dim V = s(s−1) is Cat A at those s**: exact upper bound, plus their exact lower bound. For general odd s the lower bound is open.
- *Sectors.*
  - V = A₃ ⊗ (Z/s)⁺ ⊗ (Z/(s−2))⁺ ⊕ B₁ ⊗ (Z/s)⁻ ⊗ (Z/(s−2))⁻.
  - Odd-characteristic piece: O(s)O(s−2) = (s−1)(s−3)/4.
  - Eisenstein (all local parities even): 3∏_{p^e‖n}(p^e+1)/2.
  - Model cusp = O(s)O(s−2) + 3(E(s)E(s−2) − ∏(p^e+1)/2). This gives 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 84, 110, 72, 180, 218, 132 and 758 at s = 5…25 and 41, matching N3.
- **cusp ≥ (s−1)(s−3)/4:** the dimension of the B₁ sector is Cat A. That this sector is cusp is Cat B (see Step 4).

**Step 4: which sectors are cusp. Cat B.**
- Refine W by the individual local parities Z_p = ±1. V requires ∏_{p|s} Z_p = ∏_{p|s−2} Z_p = Z₂ (Z₂ = +1 on the three even characteristics, −1 on the odd one).
- Reading via Hecke characters ψ of Q(√(s(s−2))): the local parity is ψ_p(−1) = Z_p.
  - Genus characters χ∘N have ψ_p(−1) = χ_p(N(−1)) = 1 at every ramified p. So they live exactly in the all-even sector, A₃ ⊗ ⊗_p W_p⁺. This is the Eisenstein part.
  - Any sector with some Z_p = −1 has ψ non-genus. If ψ is moreover not σ-invariant, its theta series is a dihedral Maass cusp form (Maass).
  - So cusp = (odd characteristic, odd at s and s−2) ⊕ (even characteristic, an even number of odd primes on each side, not all even).
- Status:
  - At s = 7 the odd-characteristic sector is exactly the numerical cusp labels (Closure, Cat A label match).
  - For general s, the identification "non-all-even sector = cusp" is Cat B. It needs exact ray-class confirmation, which Cusp-Onset-Closure is doing per s.
- Equality cusp = (s−1)(s−3)/4 holds exactly when E(s)E(s−2) = ∏(p^e+1)/2, i.e. when s and s−2 are both prime powers.

**Step 5: even s. Not done (partial).**
- For even s the gcd of the entries of A is 2, so L′/Z² ≅ Z/2 × Z/(s(s−2)/2) is not cyclic. The lattice is even (w_c = 0), and a ∈ L′ at s = 8.
- The odd-s proof does not transfer directly; the 2-adic module changes with s mod 8.
- The s = 2m² gap (N9) is being studied in Cusp-Onset-Closure. Their partial note: γ has a rational square root (γ+I)/(2m) in SO(A)(Q) iff s = 2m². That would give an extra symmetry and extra relations, consistent with a deficit m(m−1). I did not duplicate it. Cat B / open here.

**Step 6: consistency with the u_L counterexamples (s = 14, 17, 18, 22, 23).**
- Those statements are about the single vector u_L, not the family span. The family model gives cusp > 0 at every odd s, and its counts at 17 (110) and 23 (218) agree with n3_vs_uL.json's family_cusp_bound. So there is no conflict.
- Whether u_L meets the non-all-even sectors at those s is a u_L-specific question (Cusp-Onset-Closure). Not addressed.
