## Abstract (verbatim summary)

Treats resonant collisions between five identical ultracold bosons in the adiabatic hyperspherical representation. Quantifies the five-body recombination rate coefficient via semiclassical (WKBJ) analysis of the lowest five-body hyperspherical adiabatic potential curves in a scattering-length regime with no universal weakly bound tetramers, trimers or dimers. Compares results to the only existing experimental measurement of five-body loss in an ultracold gas of bosonic cesium atoms and to the prior theoretical estimation. Also computes the recombination rate for the process B + B + B + B + B → B4 + B in a regime containing one universal bound tetramer, using a few-channel quantum scattering calculation based on five-body hyperspherical potential curves and nonadiabatic couplings. Predicts regions where five-body recombination can dominate atom-cloud decay over 3-body and 4-body recombination, ideally testable in contemporary box traps of nearly uniform density.

## Key Theoretical Results (Higgins & Greene 2025)

- Method: Adiabatic hyperspherical representation with correlated Gaussian hyperspherical (CGHS) basis.  
- WKBJ formulas for recombination rate coefficients in the regime without universal high-lying bound states.  
- Full coupled-channel R-matrix / DVR calculation for the process into the universal tetramer + free atom.  
- Resonance positions (model units scaled to Cs):  
  – a5,1* ≈ –282(5) a0  
  – a5,2* ≈ –411(5) a0  
- Inelastic parameter η ≈ 0.45 chosen to match experimental resonance width.  
- At typical experimental densities there exist intervals of scattering length in which the five-body loss rate exceeds both three-body and four-body rates. Dominance is more pronounced at higher initial densities (e.g., 50 μm⁻³ versus 5 μm⁻³).  

## Experimental Dataset Pinned (Zenesini et al., New J. Phys. 15 043040, 2013)

The sole experimental measurement of five-body loss referenced by Higgins & Greene is the 2013 Innsbruck cesium experiment:

- Species: bosonic ¹³³Cs  
- Temperature: T = 78(3) nK (typical hold-time simulations at 80 nK)  
- Peak density: 4.2 μm⁻³  
- Trap frequency: 2π × 36.2(2) Hz  
- Initial atom number: ~6 × 10⁴  
- Scattering-length window examined: approximately –500 a0 to –200 a0 (Feshbach resonance pole at 786 G)  
- Measured resonance positions:  
  – three-body (Efimov): a_– = –955 a0  
  – four-body: a4,– = –440(10) a0 ≈ 0.46(1) a_–  
  – five-body: a5,– ≈ 0.64(2) a4,– ≈ –282 a0 (consistent with theoretical 0.65(1) a4,–)  
- Effective loss coefficients L4,eff and L5,eff extracted from atom-number decay after 100 ms hold.  
- Experimental L5,eff lies roughly a factor of 15 above the pure theoretical curve obtained with η_– = 0.20; overall scale factor applied in the original analysis.  
- Loss fractions after 100 ms: ~10 % near a ≈ –300 a0, ~35 % near a ≈ –450 a0.
