# Spin-orbit coupling in the Dirac equation

Date: 2026-10-03
Status: exploration. Not a reopening. Not a new residual-flux object.

## Where the term comes from

The Dirac operator with an electromagnetic potential is i gamma^mu (partial_mu + i e A_mu) minus m. Spin-orbit is not written in that equation. It appears after a Foldy-Wouthuysen reduction to order 1/c^2, as the term

H_SO = - (e hbar / 4 m^2 c^2) sigma · (E × p).

A companion Darwin term, (e hbar^2 / 8 m^2 c^2) div E, comes from the same reduction. The magnetic moment term, - (e hbar / 2 m) sigma · B, is the leading spin coupling and is not spin-orbit.

For a central potential this is the L·S form. A hydrogen-like scale for Z = 6 is (Z alpha)^2 times 13.6 eV = 0.026 eV. The alpha^4 m c^2 scale is 1.45 meV. A valence electron in an organic ring sits at or below the Z = 6 figure. Neither number is the 0.6 meV echo by identity.

## What the lock adds

On the lock the Dirac operator uses the Levi-Civita spin connection. Torsion does not enter. A Foldy-Wouthuysen reduction of that operator can produce a gravitational spin-orbit, or Mashhoon, term proportional to the curvature. The Mathisson-Papapetrou scale already computed for that curvature, on the conditional 137 nm rod, is 1.42 times 10^{-12} eV across a nucleon Compton length. It is 8.6 decades below 0.6 meV. The rod is unselected, so this is not a lock output.

The axial contorsion contact, 4.05 times 10^{-51} eV, is an Einstein-Cartan addition. It is not the sigma · (E × p) term, and it is off the lock.

## Split

Electromagnetic spin-orbit is the Foldy-Wouthuysen term and does not source torsion. Gravitational spin-orbit is the curvature reduction and is not a number until a rod is selected. Torsion contact is a third operator and is zero on the lock. None of the three is the phase wedge. The retired identification stands.
