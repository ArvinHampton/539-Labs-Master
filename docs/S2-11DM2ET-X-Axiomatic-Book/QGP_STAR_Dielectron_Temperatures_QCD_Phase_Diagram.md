The STAR Collaboration reported thermal dielectron measurements in Au+Au collisions at 27 GeV and 54.4 GeV in Nature Communications volume 16 article 9098 published 14 October 2025. Thermal electron-positron pairs serve as penetrating probes of the temperature of the emitting source because their invariant-mass spectra are free of strong final-state interactions and of blue-shift distortions caused by collective expansion.

Two mass windows are distinguished. In the low-mass region the spectrum is dominated by in-medium rho-zero decays. The average temperature extracted there is approximately 2.01 times 10 to the 12 kelvin. This value sits close to the chemical freeze-out temperature obtained from statistical hadron yields and to the lattice-QCD crossover temperature near 156.5 MeV. The observed yields exceed the vacuum rho-zero baseline by a factor of roughly five, indicating prolonged emission while the system remains dense near the phase boundary.

In the intermediate-mass region the spectrum is smoother and is attributed to earlier quark-antiquark annihilation inside the quark-gluon plasma. The average temperature extracted there is higher, approximately 3.25 times 10 to the 12 kelvin. The difference between the two windows maps successive stages of the system evolution: an early hotter deconfined stage followed by a later stage near the crossover to hadronic matter.

These measurements therefore supply direct experimental thermometers that place the collision trajectories on the QCD phase diagram at moderate baryon chemical potential. The low-mass temperatures cluster around the expected crossover line while the intermediate-mass temperatures lie deeper inside the quark-gluon-plasma region. No clear energy dependence appears within present statistical precision, consistent with the limited lever arm between 27 GeV and 54.4 GeV.
