# Microtubule catastrophe factors

Date: 2026-10-03
Status: public kinetics. Not a reopening. Not a new residual-flux object.
CORE_FREEZE unchanged. Rank 1 and Rank 3 stand.

## Two meanings

Catastrophe is a switching rate: the stochastic jump from growth to rapid shortening. A catastrophe factor is a protein that raises that rate. The two are not one object.

## The rate

Walker et al., Journal of Cell Biology 1988, recorded individual microtubules at 33 ms intervals. Catastrophe frequency fell as tubulin concentration rose. Plus-end catastrophes were slightly more frequent than minus-end. Rapid shortening itself stayed near 733 dimers per second at the plus end and did not depend on concentration. Later work, Gardner et al. 2011, found that the probability of catastrophe grows with the age of the microtubule, so a single frequency is an incomplete description.

The 33 ms frame is the camera interval. It is not the 25 ms Orch-OR moment. The factor 33/25 = 1.32 is a neighborhood and is refused.

## The proteins

MCAK, a kinesin-13, raises catastrophe frequency in an ATP-dependent manner at substoichiometric concentration. It is reported not to change the growth or shrinkage speed in the same way it changes the switch. Kip3, a kinesin-8, shortens the length reached before catastrophe. Stathmin sequesters tubulin and promotes disassembly. These are biochemical regulators. None is a torsion source. None is k ell = 0.02531.

## Against the overlay

The 23 ms shortening time of the 137 nm rod used the Walker shrinkage speed, not the catastrophe frequency. A factor changes how often shortening starts. It does not set the 18 fs baseline, the 137 nm rod, or 539. The retired identification stands.
