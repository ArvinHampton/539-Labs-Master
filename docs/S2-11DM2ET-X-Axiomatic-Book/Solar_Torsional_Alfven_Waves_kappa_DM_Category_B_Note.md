1. Purpose of this note
This note records an exploratory examination of the Nature Astronomy paper published 24 October 2025 (Morton et al.) reporting evidence for small-scale torsional Alfvén waves in the solar corona. The examination asks whether residual Doppler signatures and multi-spacecraft power spectra can be interpreted, under the S²-11DM²ET-X continuum energy-leakage terms, as containing a preferred harmonic near the model’s G4 ≈ 539.9 s clock. All such interpretations are Category B. No claim is made that the observational data independently confirm the continuum clock or that physics has been unified.

2. Summary of the published observations (external, Category A with respect to the paper itself)
The Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope Cryo-NIRSP instrument obtained sit-and-stare spectroscopic observations of the Fe XIII 1074.7 nm line on 30 October 2023. After removal of the kink-mode contribution at the centre of bright flux tubes, residual Doppler velocity maps show anti-phase red/blue signatures on opposite sides of the tubes. These signatures are consistent with torsional Alfvén (n = 0) motions.

Key published results:
- The torsional motions are prevalent across the observed quiet-Sun and open-field regions.
- They are broadband and stochastic; wave packets typically last one to a few cycles.
- Line-of-sight root-mean-square amplitudes are small (approximately 0.32 km/s). After correction for line-of-sight integration effects the authors estimate true amplitudes of order 19–20 km/s.
- Combined with previously measured kink-mode energy fluxes the authors place a conservative estimate of 100–400 W m⁻² for the total Alfvénic energy flux, sufficient to contribute to quiet-Sun heating and fast solar-wind acceleration.
- Power spectra of the residual Doppler signals have the same shape as the kink-mode spectra and as solar-wind Alfvénic fluctuations; no preferred monochromatic period is reported.

3. Model overlay (Category B)
Under the S²-11DM²ET-X continuum terms the residual Doppler time series were examined for the possible presence of a harmonic near 540 s (the numerical value used for G4 in the model equations). Internal exploratory calculations produced candidate power ridges near 540 s and its low-order harmonics together with quantitative estimates of a dark-matter flux modulator κ_DM. These calculations are model-dependent overlays. They do not constitute independent empirical detection of a 540 s solar period.

The same exploratory framework was applied to publicly available time series from Parker Solar Probe, SOHO LASCO, STEREO, Ulysses, Voyager and WIND. Weak ridges or coherence near the same period appear in some wavelet transforms; again these are internal model overlays and are not claimed as peer-reviewed detections.

The Nature Astronomy 2025 paper supplies high-quality observational evidence that small-scale torsional Alfvén waves are a continuous feature of the quiet corona.
