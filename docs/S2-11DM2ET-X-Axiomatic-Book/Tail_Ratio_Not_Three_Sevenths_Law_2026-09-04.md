# Tail Ratio Is Not Three Sevenths

Date: 2026-09-04
Pack+(S). Residual-flux provenance mandatory. CORE_FREEZE holds.

Claim tested: tail A over tail B at the first-odd probe equals 3/7 as a law.
Decision: not a law.

## Identity

The ratio depends on one combination z = shift times square root of pi y.
Ratio = erfc(square root of 2 times z) / erfc(z).

## Cited point

Shift 4.5, y = 0.01.
z = 0.797604
Ratio = 0.426741
3/7 = 0.428571
Miss = 0.00183, about four tenths of a percent.

Shift 4.5 is distinguished: n=1 and a=7/2.
y = 0.01 is not distinguished. It is a round probe height. It is not 1/7, 1/35, 1/539, or ln 3 / 539.

To make the ratio exact at shift 4.5 requires y = 0.009938.
To make it exact at y = 0.01 requires shift 4.486.

## Why it looked close

That height puts the first odd shift on the Gaussian constant square root of 2 over pi. Two constants plus one convenient hundredth. The hundredth was chosen in the probe.

At the same y the other first-sector shifts do not hit 1/7 or 5/7.
Leftover heights at shift 4.5 all miss 3/7 by large margins.

## Type

3/7 is an x-coordinate of a Fricke sample.
The tail ratio is a function of y and of a Fourier shift.
Every number between 0 and 1 is hit by exactly one z. Invertibility is not selection.

Slash_two stays empty. A tail-ratio identity would relate two conventions. It would not be the two-sided factor that matches sample magnitudes of C.
